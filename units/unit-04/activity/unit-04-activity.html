Unit 04 activity

Where Does It Stack?

Pairs40 minutesNot graded

Style a small card layout of your own (three or four cards, any content you like) with flexbox and at least one CSS custom property.

The decision you have to make

On a narrow, phone-width screen, do your cards stack to one column, or stay two? Pick a real breakpoint and be able to say why there and not somewhere else.

Resize your actual browser window narrow rather than guessing at a number; the right breakpoint is the one where two columns genuinely stop looking good, not a round number picked in advance.

AI use on this activity: Level 1

You may ask an AI tool general questions about JavaScript, Express, or HTTP. It may not see your code, your terminal output, or the assignment text, and it may never tell you what to write.

Where a layout should break is a judgment call made by looking at a real screen, which is exactly the skill this activity builds. Asking a tool for 'the standard breakpoint' skips the looking. Level 1 is the same level every Lab in this course uses, for the same reason: this is foundational skill-building, not shipped work.

How to work

  • Driver types. Navigator reads, questions, and looks things up.
  • Swap at the midpoint, but only when I tell you to. Do not swap on your own clock. Both of you write real code; whoever only watched cannot do the homework, so the swap is enforced from the front of the room, not left to pairs to remember.
  • One machine is fine. Two is fine. Talking is the part that matters.

Starting point

Start from last class's public/style.css. Add a custom property and a flex container:

:root {
  --gap: 1rem;
}

.cards {
  display: flex;
  flex-wrap: wrap;
  gap: var(--gap);
}

.card {
  padding: 1rem;
  border: 1px solid var(--color-border, #ccc);
  box-sizing: border-box;
}

Then your breakpoint, once you have decided it:

@media (max-width: 480px) {
  .card { flex-basis: 100%; }
}

The thing that will get you

A correct <link> tag and a real stylesheet can still produce zero visible change if the class name in the HTML and the selector in the CSS do not match exactly, including case. Check the actual class on the element before assuming the file is not loading.

Done when

  1. Three or four cards lay out with flexbox and wrap correctly.
  2. You have a real, chosen breakpoint, tested by actually resizing the window.
  3. You have compared your answer with a neighbouring pair, but only once I say it is time; that comparison is its own short phase, not something to rush into once your code works.

You will need this for your exit ticket

Before you leave, you will be asked to name one CSS rule you personally wrote and one rule your partner wrote. Keep track as you go, driver and navigator both, so you are not reconstructing it from memory at the door.

If you finish early

Add a second custom property (a color, not just a spacing value) and use it in at least two places. Then try the Home Exercise's :has() dark-mode toggle on your own layout, no JavaScript, just a checkbox and CSS.
